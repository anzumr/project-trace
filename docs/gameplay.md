# Offline practice

Reference-model update: the practice bot starts as a slim red capsule body with a separate gray spherical head and a small visible gap. Its starting dimensions are 0.5 m wide by 1.8 m tall. Body hits deal 20 HP/s. The gray head is a distinct hit region dealing 2× damage (40 HP/s). When a ray intersects both regions, the head takes priority and damage is applied once. Cover still blocks both. The final micro-orb has no separate head and deals normal body damage. The HUD identifies confirmed practice headshots. Hit tests use the capsule plus head sphere, transformed by the same scale as the visible model. The gap and empty rounded corners do not count as hits. Existing 360-degree movement, size/speed upgrades, health billboard and final micro-orb remain. Online opponents and lobby previews share this model, uniformly scaled by the selected mode.

Online Phase 2 uses these same rules with server authority. Both players ready for a three-second countdown and a 90-second round. Symmetric spawns reset to (+5,+9) and (-5,-9); the central barrier initially blocks their direct ray. First KO or higher HP at timeout wins a round; first to two round wins ends the match. Tied timeouts enter 15-second sudden death. First confirmed hit wins; simultaneous hits or no hits replay the round. Simultaneous KO also replays without points. A three-second summary precedes the next countdown. Both can ready for a full match rematch. The server owns HP and hit confirmation. Escape disables your input but does not pause the opponent. Disconnect immediately forfeits. Scores, timing and results come from server snapshots.

Camera is first person, eye height 1.6 m, vertical FOV 85 degrees. Screen-center reticle aligns with camera forward; mouse orientation updates each render without smoothing. Optional raw pointer movement falls back to ordinary pointer lock. Pointer unlock, tab hiding and window blur clear inputs and stop damage. Space is edge-triggered, preventing held-key bunny hopping.

Shared config sets 100 HP, 20 DPS, 60 Hz simulation, 7 m/s movement, gravity 23 m/s² and jump impulse 8 m/s. The practice target follows a smooth 360-degree circuit with varying radius around the arena center. It moves in X and Z, traverses all quadrants and passes behind the original player spawn. Its 7–11 m radius clears both arena walls and central cover. HP only changes while Mouse 1 is held and the forward ray intersects its exact box before any solid cover. Releasing Mouse 1, pausing, losing focus or resetting clears firing. A camera-facing health bar hovers above the target and shrinks with remaining HP. This user-requested hold-to-fire rule supersedes the original automatic tracking rule. Each defeated bot respawns after 0.65 seconds with 100 HP. Wave 2 increases movement speed; wave 3 shrinks the model without changing speed; those upgrades alternate. Speed rises 15% per speed upgrade, capped at 3×. Each size upgrade multiplies width by 0.90 and height by 0.82, with a 0.4 m minimum. Wave 17 becomes a 0.4 m spherical bot with a true sphere collider. Further waves continue indefinitely at that size and speed. Movement phase stays continuous across upgrades; Escape pauses movement and respawn. Restart progression resets to wave 1. There is no match timer or round score in practice.

Arena interior is 28 × 28 m. Shared collision prevents leaving the perimeter and penetrating cover; vertical collision supports floors, solid tops and ceilings. Movement uses axis-separated body-box collision. Catch-up is capped to 100 ms per render after stalls, so long stalls do not suddenly apply seconds of damage.

Scale changes target width/height, controller radius/height and eye height together. Speed multiplies the target horizontal velocity. Jump multiplier applies to initial vertical impulse; peak height follows gravity, so it is not a direct height multiplier. Server physics and client prediction use the same shared parameters. Multiplayer uses the same capsule/head hit regions: 20 HP/s on the body and 40 HP/s on the gray head. All players use the host-selected profile, with new approval required after any change. Practice progression and its capsule/head model remain separate.
